_At a dive resort, guests rent kit every day. A regulator that went in for service yesterday should not be handed out this morning just because the spreadsheet still says it is free._

## Daily Rentals, Constant Turnover

A resort rents gear to new guests on most days of the week: wetsuits, BCDs, regulators, fins. Some guests rent for one dive, others for a full week in a Stay & Dive package. Alongside that, items go out for service or repair, and come back on their own schedule.

When availability lives in a spreadsheet or a whiteboard, the gear room and the front desk are working from two different pictures. A unit sent for repair is still counted as available until someone remembers to update the sheet. The first sign of the gap is often a guest at the counter and no kit in their size.

## Bookings Draw Down From Real Availability

In RidgeHQ, rental gear is tracked by unit and size, and rentals are booked against that availability. When a booking includes rental gear, it draws from the units actually available in the size the guest needs, rather than from a single headcount per gear type.

That only works if availability is honest. A unit that is booked, out with a guest or out for service is not available, and the system has to know which is which without anyone re-keying it.

## The Maintenance Hold

Putting an item under maintenance takes it out of rental availability. While it is held, it is not offered for rental, so neither a front-desk booking nor an online booking can draw on it.

The hold stays in place until the item's return to service is confirmed in RidgeHQ. Getting the unit back from the workshop is not enough on its own: someone has to confirm it is ready, and only then does it become available to rent again. That confirmation step is the control point. It separates "the regulator is back on the shelf" from "the regulator is ready to go on a guest".

For a resort, this matters because the cost of getting it wrong lands on a guest who has paid for a holiday. Keeping held items out of availability means the gear room and the booking system agree on what can be handed out today.

## What This Is, and What It Is Not

The maintenance hold is an availability control for rental gear. It is not a full equipment-management suite. RidgeHQ does not claim automated service schedules, maintenance reminders, serial-number tracking or tank counts as part of this feature. Your technicians still decide when an item needs servicing; RidgeHQ makes sure that decision is reflected in what can be rented.

Boats are handled separately in the fleet module, which covers capacity, crew and inspection dates.

## Key takeaways

- Rental gear is tracked by unit and size, and bookings draw on real availability.
- Gear under maintenance is not offered for rental while it is held.
- An item becomes rentable again only once its return to service is confirmed.
- The hold is an availability control, not automated service scheduling.
