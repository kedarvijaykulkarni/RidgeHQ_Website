_A single instructor and a shared calendar works fine. The moment you're matching bookings to staff by certification, language, and a ratio requirement — at volume, under time pressure — a whiteboard or a group chat stops being a scheduling system and becomes a source of mistakes._

## What actually breaks

The failure mode isn't "we don't have a schedule." It's that the schedule doesn't know what a booking actually requires. A ski school's 8:45 a.m. rush is families wanting privates and group classes, at different levels, in different languages, all needing an instructor assignment in the same few minutes. An outdoor/whitewater trip needs enough guides to meet a ratio, on top of enough boats and shuttle seats for the same headcount.

When the booking and the staff roster live in separate places — a widget or spreadsheet for one, a whiteboard or chat thread for the other — someone has to hold the requirements in their head and match by memory, at exactly the moment there's the least time to double-check.

## Why this is a scheduling problem, not a staffing problem

More staff doesn't fix this by itself. The problem is that a booking's requirements (level, language, ratio) and a staff member's qualifications (certification, language, availability) aren't connected data — they're two lists a person reconciles manually. Adding staff adds more entries to reconcile, not less work per assignment.
