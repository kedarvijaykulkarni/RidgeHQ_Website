_Most ski school bookings are made by a parent for children who will never touch the booking form. The waiver still has to name each child, and it has to be done before the 8:45 rush at the meeting point._

## The Parent Books, the Children Ski

A typical family booking is three children at three levels in three group classes, sometimes with a private lesson for a parent on top. On paper, that is a stack of forms at the desk, filled in with gloves on, while the instructors wait at the meeting point. Forms get signed once for the family instead of once per child, or end up filed against the wrong class.

In RidgeHQ, waivers are built in, not a paid add-on. Waiver requirements attach to lesson products, and every participant on the booking completes the waiver with a typed e-signature before checkout can be submitted. A family booking three children produces three signed records, one per student, each stored with the exact text agreed to and the answers given.

There is no dedicated guardian-signature flow today. You can add questions to the template for a parent or guardian to complete, but RidgeHQ does not verify the signer's relationship to the child.

## Questions That Fit the Student

A five-year-old in ski kindergarten and a teenager joining an off-piste clinic should not answer the same form. Waiver templates support conditional questions: a follow-up appears only when an earlier answer calls for it, so the clinic can ask about previous off-piste experience without burdening the beginner group. Lesson products can also require a minimum level, which the booking form asks for only where the class needs it, and the roster shows each student's level.

The same template can carry a medical questionnaire. Medical answers need a separate health-data consent, are visible only to senior roles, and every read is audited.

## Multi-Day Courses Without Daily Paperwork

A five-morning course is sold as one booking with dated sessions across the week, so each student signs at checkout for that booking rather than at the desk every morning. Attendance is tracked across the course and completion is recorded per student. When a student needs to sign outside checkout, for example a walk-in added at the desk, staff can capture the waiver directly or email a single-use renewal link that expires after seven days.

## Check-In at the Meeting Point

Check-in is live. Instructors and desk staff check students in from the session roster in the Event Planner, the same roster that shows each student's level. Parents can check in every child on their confirmed booking by scanning a per-booking QR code. The QR code is for check-in only (QR waiver signing is not built); waivers are signed at checkout, by renewal link, or with staff at the desk. RidgeHQ records the arrival time and whether staff or the guest checked in, so the instructor knows who is actually standing at the flag. For the full detail on waiver evidence and medical data, see [built-in waiver management](/blog/waiver-system-dive-center-operations).

## Key takeaways

- Waivers are built into RidgeHQ's ski school software and enforced per student with a typed e-signature before checkout.
- A parent booking for several children produces one signed record per child; there is no dedicated guardian-signature flow.
- Conditional questions, level requirements and a restricted, audited medical questionnaire fit the form to the class.
- Multi-day courses are one booking, and staff and guest QR check-in work at the meeting point.
